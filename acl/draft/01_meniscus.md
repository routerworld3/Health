The **meniscus** is a **C-shaped rubbery cushion inside the knee**.

Each knee has **two menisci**:

1. **Medial meniscus** — inside part of the knee
2. **Lateral meniscus** — outside part of the knee

Think of it like a **shock absorber pad** between the thigh bone and shin bone.

```text
Thigh bone
   ↓
[ Meniscus cushion ]
   ↑
Shin bone
```

## What does the meniscus do?

The meniscus helps:

* Absorb shock when walking
* Protect the knee cartilage
* Keep the knee stable
* Help smooth bending and twisting movement
* Spread body weight evenly across the knee

## What is a meniscus tear?

A **meniscus tear** means that cushion has a crack or split.

In this MRI, the report says there is a **Grade III tear in the anterior horn of the lateral meniscus**, with a small **parameniscal cyst** near it. That means the tear is on the **front part of the outer-side meniscus**. 

## Why does bending hurt?

When the knee bends, squats, twists, or goes up/down stairs, the torn meniscus can get **pinched** between the bones.

That can cause:

* Pain with bending
* Pain with squatting
* Clicking
* Catching
* Swelling
* Feeling stuck or locked
* Pain when turning/pivoting

## Can someone still walk with a meniscus tear?

Yes. Many people can **stand and walk** with a meniscus tear, especially if it is not locked. But bending, squatting, stairs, or twisting may hurt.

That fits what you described: **she can stand and walk, but bending the knee causes pain.**

## Simple summary

The **meniscus is the knee’s cushion/shock absorber**.
This MRI shows a tear in the **outer-front meniscus**, which can explain why **bending the knee is painful even though standing and walking may still be possible**.
