The **ACL and PCL are two main stabilizing ligaments inside the knee**. They cross each other in the center of the knee, which is why they are called **cruciate ligaments**.

In this MRI report, the patient has a **high partial ACL tear involving about 70–80% of fibers** and a **partial PCL tear**.

## ACL: Anterior Cruciate Ligament

The **ACL** is in the front/center part of the knee. Its job is to stop the shin bone from sliding too far **forward** and to help control twisting/pivoting.

### ACL problem can cause:

* Knee feels like it may **give out**
* Trouble with twisting or turning
* Pain and swelling after activity
* Fear of falling
* Difficulty with stairs or uneven ground
* More stress on the meniscus/cartilage over time

AAOS notes that ACL treatment decisions depend on factors such as injury severity, age, joint wear/arthritis, instability symptoms, and activity level. ([OrthoInfo][1])

## PCL: Posterior Cruciate Ligament

The **PCL** is behind the ACL. Its job is to stop the shin bone from sliding too far **backward**.

### PCL problem can cause:

* Pain deep inside the knee
* Swelling
* Trouble walking normally if painful
* Pain going **downstairs**
* Feeling unstable, especially on slopes or uneven ground
* Ongoing pain/instability if it does not heal well

AAOS says PCL injuries can occur with damage to other knee structures like cartilage, other ligaments, and bone, and persistent instability or pain may need specialist treatment. ([OrthoInfo][2])

## Why she can stand but bending hurts

Standing straight may feel okay because the knee is not being compressed or twisted much. But bending increases pressure on:

* Meniscus tear
* Kneecap cartilage damage
* Arthritis area
* Swelling/fluid inside the knee

So her symptom — **standing okay, bending painful** — may be more from the **meniscus tear, chondromalacia patella, arthritis, and fluid**, not only ACL/PCL. The MRI also shows a **lateral meniscus tear, osteoarthritis, mild joint effusion, and Grade III chondromalacia patella**. 

## What problems to watch for

The most important warning signs are:

* Knee gives out or buckles
* Knee locks and cannot bend/straighten
* Increasing swelling
* Cannot bear weight
* Severe pain with stairs
* New calf swelling/pain
* Red/hot knee or fever

## Bottom line

**ACL = prevents forward slipping and twisting instability.**
**PCL = prevents backward slipping and deep knee instability.**

In this patient, both are partially torn, but because she can stand and walk, the bigger day-to-day pain with bending may be coming from the **meniscus tear + kneecap cartilage damage + arthritis + swelling**. She should still see a **knee orthopedic specialist** because multiple structures are involved.
